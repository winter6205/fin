### Exit-code contract, and how these runs sat inside it

`run_measured.sh` defines `64` usage, `66` missing input file, `65` unbound
model fetch, `75` model-cache/identity-gate refusal, and `1` when the JSON-line
count is not 1 or the container exited non-zero. **None of the five fired on any
of these four runs**; all four took the measured path and returned `0` with
`json_lines: 1`, `container_exit_code: 0` and `docker_run_exit_code: 0`. The
contract is therefore *observed to hold on the success path only* — no run here
exercised 64/66/65/75/1, and nothing in this record should be read as
evidence about those paths.

## Measurement caveat: the image changed under the only comparator

**The timings above are not comparable to the 2026-09-29 comparator, and must
not be read as if they were.** This is a confound in the comparison, not a
caveat about the measurement's own validity — every run here did what it was
run to do. Three facts, all of which a reader needs before quoting a number:

1. **The measured image carries no baked `.pyc`.** `fin-g4-pipeline:r1` sets
   `PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1` as a persistent image environment variable, and
   `run_measured.sh` starts a fresh container per run without overriding it. No
   bytecode cache can therefore survive between runs, and the 1,327 modules
   that the legacy image shipped pre-compiled are recompiled from source on
   every run. That compile is **inside** the reported `wall_clock_ms` (the
   driver checkpoints before `build_converter`), so the per-run cost is already
   in every number in the table above.
2. **The comparator was taken on the legacy image.** The committed
   `2022_Q3_MSFT_20260929T130258Z.meta.json` predates the repair, so the two
   runs differ in the image as well as in the code path. The image itself
   changed: 589,605,751 B → 579,825,559 B (9,780,192 B smaller) and 1,099
   `.pyc` removed under `site-packages`. Any latency delta is therefore
   **confounded by the image change and cannot be attributed to the `.pyc`
   change alone** — the two are the same change, and nothing here separates
   them.
3. **The measured direction is the opposite of the naive prediction, and that
   is not evidence that bytecode compilation is free.** `total_latency_ms`
   moved 1,003,027.5 → 590,202.7, a **~41 % decrease**. Per-run compilation
   should cost time, so a reader could take this as showing the compile is
   negligible. It does not show that. The decrease is dominated by the image
   change and by host conditions on a different date; the compile is real, it
   is inside the measured number, and this comparison cannot size it. Treat the
   ~41 % as unexplained-by-construction, not as a measurement of anything
   except "these two runs were not the same measurement".

**What survives the caveat:** the content-bearing comparison is unaffected,
because it does not depend on the image bytes or on timing. The 80-page run's
844/56/0 regions, 250,253 native characters, 0 OCR, `SUCCESS` and no
`page_limit_exceeded` are identical to the comparator, and that identity is the
finding. The timing rows are retained in the table for completeness and are
marked here as not a latency result.

## Findings

**1 — the 80-page case's content outputs are bit-reproducible; its timings are
not comparable at all.** Every content-bearing field is identical to the
2026-09-29 run across 80 pages: 844/56/0 regions, 250253 native characters, 0
OCR, SUCCESS, no `page_limit_exceeded`. That identity is the finding. The
timing side is weaker than "not reproducible": total latency moved 1003.0 s →
590.2 s and the whole-document phase fell 40 %, but those two runs were taken
on **different images**, so the delta is confounded and is not a latency result
at all — see *Measurement caveat* above for why, and specifically for why the
direction of that change is not evidence that the per-run `.pyc` compile is
free. The content result is consistent with the C2 acceptance's "do not require
byte-identical telemetry such as timestamps or observed resource peaks"; this
lane can support a claim about *outputs* and must not support one about
*durations*.

**2 — the `native_text_chars` numbers differ from the manifest by definition,
not by corruption.** The manifest defines its figure as the length of poppler
`pdftotext` output for that page after stripping whitespace
(`expectation_basis.field_definitions`), and its `expectation_basis.rule` states
every expectation was produced with tools outside fin, with fin's parser never
consulted. The driver's `native_text_char_count` is its own extraction. The two
counters are not the same measurement, and the manifest never claimed fin would
reproduce the poppler figure — so 1882 vs 1860, and 460/1111/337 vs
449/1181/334, are **not** defects and must not be "fixed" by changing either
side. They are recorded here as mismatches because the numbers are not equal and
a reader is entitled to know why.

**3 — region kinds disagree with the manifest's floor on both raster- and
vector-borne cases.** The scanned pages produced 0 text regions and 4 table
boxes, including a table on the prose page p2; the mixed p2 statement page
produced no table at all. Both directions are wrong relative to a human
labelling, so this is not a systematic "Heron calls everything a table"
bias — on the vector-born mixed p2 it under-called. `known_gaps` item 8 leaves
Docling/Heron region output explicitly to the T2a run, and item 5 says no
box-level ground truth is committed, so nothing here scores a region count
against gold. The floor disagreement is a finding for whoever owns the
page/region budget, and it is the kind of finding that matters for a vision-call
budget computed from region kinds.

**4 — the mixed p3 OCR expectation is unmet by capability, not by error.** The
manifest sets `ocr_expected: true` on p3 but scopes it precisely: "the figure
region only … the chart content requires OCR". The driver has no region-scoped
OCR step; its rule is per-page, `detect_ocr_required(min_text_chars=1)`, and p3
has 337 native characters, so the rule correctly says no. Running whole-page
OCR on a page that has a text layer would be the wrong behaviour, not the
right one. The expectation is therefore out of reach for this lane as written,
and belongs with whoever implements figure-scoped preparation.

**5 — the record shape cannot verify one of the gold quotes.** `ocr_text_sample`
is capped at 160 characters per page, which is enough for the two heading quotes
and far too short for the p3 numeric row the manifest calls "the
fixture-independent check that OCR did not drop digits". Either the sample
window grows or that quote is checked by a different route. Until then, p3's
digit-fidelity claim is **unverified**, not failed.

**6 — `rendered_dpi` is null on every page of every run**, with
`rendered_dpi_error: "AttributeError: 'PdfBitmap' object has no attribute
'get_dpi'"`, while the bitmaps are exactly 200 dpi by arithmetic (1700 × 2200
px for the 612 × 792 pt letter pages; 1653 × 2339 px for the 594.96 × 841.92 pt
A4 pages). It is recorded per page and is not a `harness_failures` entry, so it
does not fail the run — but any consumer reading `rendered_dpi` as the budget
input it is named for gets `null` instead of `200`. Worth a ticket against the
pypdfium2 pin.

**7 — the host-side cgroup peak was unavailable.** `host_side_cgroup_memory_peak_bytes`
and `host_side_cgroup_memory_swap_peak_bytes` are `null` on all four runs: the
out-of-band probe in `run_measured.sh:388-394` looks for
`/sys/fs/cgroup/docker/<id>` and `…/system.slice/docker-<id>.scope`, and neither
was readable under this WSL2 host. The driver-side `cgroup_peak_bytes` is the
reading that stands; the out-of-band cross-check that exists precisely to
survive a driver death did not fire here.

## C2 — deferred, not passed

**C2 remains DEFERRED and UNPASSED. Nothing in this record closes it.**

`plans/post321-pipeline-reproducibility.md` C2 requires the gold on **dedicated
4 GB hardware**, and its own acceptance forbids the substitution this task was
close to making: "If the required device is unavailable, record the expected
command and missing prerequisite as blocked evidence; do not substitute a larger
host with cgroup limits or close C2." The operator phase ruling of 2026-09-30
keeps that leg as target-deployment evidence while VM preparation proceeds.

- **Expected command, unchanged from C2's acceptance:** the same four
  invocations, e.g.
  `G4_WORKTREE=<checkout> G4_IMAGE=<pinned> bash scripts/g4_pipeline/run_measured.sh data/corpus/g4_attachment_gold/g4-scanned-001.pdf`
  plus the mixed, native-text and `data/corpus/pdf/2022_Q3_MSFT.pdf` cases, run
  on the dedicated device, each expected to return `0` with `json_lines: 1`.
- **Missing prerequisite:** the dedicated 4 GB device itself. This host is
  4 CPUs / 7.66 GiB with ~3.3 GiB available; the `4g` in every row above is a
  `--memory` argument the harness supplies by default, not the device.
- **What this record may be used for:** comparable VM evidence that the pinned
  pipeline reads all four committed PDFs to completion under its own documented
  ceiling. **What it may not be used for:** closing C2, asserting the 4 GB
  target-device envelope, or asserting the 80-page peak (3.11 GiB observed under
  a 4 GiB cap) as a device measurement.
